    Help
    
Each section is one piece of the line, and the sections join end to start in order. Pick a type for each: a Bézier curve with handles, a Sine piece, a Flat stretch or an instant Jump. Everything is kept in this browser as you go.

    
Click a section in the preview, or its number under the preview, to edit it on its own; its controls appear beside (or below) the preview. The selected section is drawn bold with its hollow Bézier handles; drag any dot to edit. Dark dots mark where sections join. The square marks the start. It can’t be dragged: the wave loops forever, so the start always sits at the same height as the end, and moving the last dot moves the square with it. The thin line running across is the middle line, and the dashed lines mark the limit of 1 above and below it.

    
Play plays the line as one repeating cycle; changing the frequency changes the pitch, even while it plays. Undo and Redo also work with Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y (⌘Z and ⌘⇧Z on a Mac). Scale to fit moves the wave so the start square sits on the middle line, then makes it as large as fits between −1 and 1, without changing its shape.

    
Save downloads the wave as a small .dial.json file; send it to anyone and they can open it here and carry on editing. You can also drop a wave file anywhere on the page to open it. The * next to the name (and in the browser tab) means the wave has changes since it was last saved or opened; if it does, opening another wave or starting a new one asks whether to save it first.

    
A Sine section is part of a sine wave from its start height to its end height: “Both ends” is half a cycle, smooth at both ends, and “End only” or “Start only” is a quarter cycle that is smooth at that end. Two “Both ends” sections, low to high and back, make one exact sine cycle. A Flat section stays at its start height, and a Jump has no length. Length can be any number from 0 up. Length and handle x are measured from the section’s start. The handles are kept where the curve can never go backwards: Handle 1 x can reach up to 4/3 of the length and Handle 2 x down to −1/3 of it, but how far each one can go depends on where the other is. If a shorter length leaves the handles no room, they move in towards the start just enough to fit. Every y value is the height above or below the middle line. End y goes from −1 to 1. The handles can go further, as far as the curve itself stays between −1 and 1; how far depends on the rest of the section.
