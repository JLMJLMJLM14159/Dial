Reference accepts a single-cycle WAV (one cycle only, up to 4,096 samples) or a saved wave (.dial.json).

Harmonics

How strong each harmonic is, compared with the strongest. The 1st is the note itself, the 2nd is twice its frequency, and so on. Smooth shapes have few harmonics and sound soft; sharp corners and jumps have many and sound bright. This is only a view: edit the wave above.

Keyboard and MIDI

Press, tap or slide across the keys to play. On a computer keyboard, hold A W S E D F T G Y H U J K O L P ; to play notes starting from C4; Z and X move down and up an octave (the piano follows).

A MIDI keyboard’s sustain pedal holds notes, and its pitch-bend wheel bends notes by up to 2 semitones.

Export

The cycle is one repeat of the shape, exactly as drawn, for loading into a wavetable synth or sampler. The note is the current frequency held for the note length (up to 60 seconds), to drop onto a track in a DAW.

Data (JSON) 

    
In your own code, call getDialValues() to get this same data.

  


  

    Help
    
Each section is one piece of the line, and the sections join end to start in order. Pick a type for each: a Bézier curve with handles, a Sine piece, a straight Line, or a Curve (a line with a bend). Everything is kept in this browser as you go.

    
Click a section in the preview, or its number under the preview, to edit it on its own; its controls appear beside (or below) the preview. The selected section is drawn bold with its hollow Bézier handles; drag any dot to edit. Dark dots mark where sections join. The square marks the start. It can’t be dragged: the wave loops forever, so the start always sits at the same height as the end, and moving the last dot moves the square with it. The thin line running across is the middle line, and the dashed lines mark the limit of 1 above and below it.

    
Play (or the Space bar) plays the line as one repeating cycle; changing the frequency changes the pitch, even while it plays. Presets, next to Open, starts from a standard Sine, Saw, Triangle or Square wave. Press ? (or the ? button under the preview) for a list of every keyboard shortcut. Undo and Redo also work with Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y (⌘Z and ⌘⇧Z on a Mac). With nothing typed into, ← and → select the previous or next section, ↑ and ↓ move its end (by 0.05, or 0.01 with Shift), [ and ] shorten or lengthen it (by 0.1, or 0.01 with Shift), Delete removes it, and Q turns Snap on or off. Snap makes dragged dots land on a grid (every 0.25 across, every 0.1 in height). Ghost copies shows faint copies of the cycles before and after. Reference… shows a single-cycle WAV (one cycle only, at most 4,096 samples; 2,048 is usual) or a saved wave as a faint shape behind yours to trace over; it's only a guide and isn't saved with your wave, and × clears it. Symmetry makes the second half of the cycle the first half upside down (only odd harmonics); the last end is then linked to the opposite of the start's height. Scale to fit moves the wave so the start square sits on the middle line, then makes it as large as fits between −1 and 1, without changing its shape.

    
The two menus at the end of the toolbar pick a colour theme and Light, Dark or Auto (which follows your device). Link copies a web address that contains the whole wave (nothing is uploaded anywhere); whoever opens it gets the wave, just like opening a file. Save downloads the wave as a small .dial.json file; send it to anyone and they can open it here and carry on editing. You can also drop a wave file anywhere on the page to open it. The * next to the name (and in the browser tab) means the wave has changes since it was last saved or opened; if it does, opening another wave or starting a new one asks whether to save it first.

    
A Sine section is part of a sine wave from its start height to its end height: “Both ends” is half a cycle, smooth at both ends, and “End only” or “Start only” is a quarter cycle that is smooth at that end. Two “Both ends” sections, low to high and back, make one exact sine cycle. A Line goes straight from its start to its end: keep End y at the start’s height for a level stretch, or set Length to 0 for an instant jump (as in square and saw waves). A Curve is a line with a Bend from −20 to 20: 0 is straight, a positive bend moves fast at first and levels off into the end (like a pluck dying away or a charging curve), and a negative bend starts slowly and speeds up. The bend is the rate of an exponential: from height 1 to e⁻⁴ ≈ 0.0183 with Bend 4 is exactly e^(−4x). Length can be any number from 0 up. Length and handle x are measured from the section’s start. The handles are kept where the curve can never go backwards: Handle 1 x can reach up to 4/3 of the length and Handle 2 x down to −1/3 of it, but how far each one can go depends on where the other is. If a shorter length leaves the handles no room, they move in towards the start just enough to fit. Every y value is the height above or below the middle line. End y goes from −1 to 1. The handles can go further, as far as the curve itself stays between −1 and 1; how far depends on the rest of the section.
